What is Puja?

A Key Stage 3 Guide to Hindu Worship

Puja is the act of showing reverence, devotion, and honour to a deity, a spirit, or another aspect of the divine through prayers, songs, and rituals. For Hindus, it is a primary way of making an active, personal spiritual connection with God.

Where Puja Takes Place

Puja can be performed in two main locations, depending on whether the worship is a private daily routine or a larger community gathering:

  • The Mandir (Temple): Public worship often led by a priest (Brahmin). These rituals are typically large, highly structured, and bring the wider Hindu community together.
  • The Home Shrine: Most Hindu families maintain a dedicated small shrine or altar at home. Home Puja is usually performed daily by family members, integrating faith directly into everyday life.

The Five Senses in Puja

A core concept in Hindu worship is the engagement of all five senses to focus the mind entirely on the divine, pushing away worldly distractions. This is achieved using specific items arranged on a Puja tray:

1. Sight (Darshan)

Worshippers look directly into the eyes of the Murti (the sacred image, statue, or icon of the deity) to see God and to feel seen by God. Small oil lamps called diyas are circled to provide light.

2. Hearing

A small bell (Ghanti) is rung to clear the mind, ward off negative energy, and announce one's presence to the deity. Worshippers also chant mantras and sing hymns known as Bhajans.

3. Smell

Incense sticks (Agarbatti) are burned to purify the surrounding air, create a distinct spiritual atmosphere, and represent prayers lifting up towards the heavens.

4. Touch

Worshippers bow down or gently touch the base of the Murti out of deep respect. They also wave their hands over the holy flame of the lamp and touch their own eyes and forehead to absorb its warmth and blessings.

5. Taste

Food offerings such as fresh fruit, traditional sweets, water, and nuts are presented directly to the deity. Once the offering is accepted and blessed, this holy food is distributed to the worshippers as Prashad.

The Aarti Ceremony

The climax of many Puja rituals is the Aarti ceremony. During Aarti, a decorative plate holding a lamp with burning wicks (often soaked in clarified butter, or ghee) is rotated in a clockwise direction in front of the Murti. While the lamp is circled, the congregation sings traditional prayers accompanied by bells, drums, and musical instruments, filling the space with light and sound.

The Core Purpose of Puja

Within Hindu philosophy, performing Puja serves several profound spiritual and social functions:

  • It fosters a direct, loving relationship (Bhakti) between the individual worshipper and God.
  • It reinforces family structures and social cohesion through shared community participation.
  • It helps an individual fulfill their religious duty (Dharma) and accumulate positive actions, which influences their Karma.
