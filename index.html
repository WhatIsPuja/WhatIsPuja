Puja & Hindu Worship

Exploring Traditions, Senses, and Shrines in Hinduism

What’s Puja?

Puja is a part of Hinduism; it’s a ceremonial worship that can range from daily rites to full on home rituals. Usually on the day you celebrate puja, October 16 to October 21, you offer flowers to a statue or an image of a God.

Puja can vary a lot for different people. Different communities in different regions and even times of day can change the ritual. You even need different types of worshipers and different religious texts.

When celebrating puja, the image you worship must be treated like a human e.g being put to bed and cradles to sleep. The different types of worship can decide who does the worship; what I mean is sometimes just the worshipper can do the service alone but others require priests. It can also be worshiped as an act of devotion.

Puja is celebrated because it is believed it protects you from coming sorrows in life and brings spiritual enlightenment.

How Hindus Worship & Puja Items

When Hindus worship at a shrine, it is called puja and it uses all five senses to help them feel close to God.

  • Sense of SmellFirst, they use their sense of smell by lighting incense sticks, which makes the room smell nice and feel peaceful.
  • Sense of TouchNext, they use their sense of touch by putting red kum kum powder on their foreheads for good luck and blessing and by counting mala beads while they say prayers.
  • Sense of SightFor their sense of sight, they do arti by waving a small arti lamp with a flame in front of the deity to show respect.
  • Sense of TasteFinally, after giving food offerings to the deity, they use their sense of taste by eating prashad, which is the special blessed food shared at the end.

Shaivism

I am writing about Shaivism. Shaivism is a major branch of Hinduism where followers (known as Shaivites) worship Shiva as the Supreme Being. In a Shaivite shrine, either at home or in a temple (mandir), worshippers focus their daily prayers and rituals, puja, on Shiva to seek spiritual peace.

Here are some ways Shiva is different from other traditions: Shaivism is worshipped by meditation, yoga and asceticism, whereas Shaktism worships by chanting or going to festivals.
