What’s Puja?

Puja is a part of Hinduism; it’s a ceremonial worship that can range from daily rites to full on home rituals. Usually on the day you celebrate puja, October 16 to October 21, you offer flowers to a statue or an image of a God. Puja can vary a lot for different people. Different communities in different regions and even times of day can change the ritual. You even need different types of worshipers and different religious texts. When celebrating puja, the image you worship must be treated like a human e.g being put to bed and cradles to sleep. The different types of worship can decide who does the worship; what I mean is sometimes just the worshipper can do the service alone but others require priests. It can also be worshiped as an act of devotion.

Puja is celebrated because it is believed it protects you from coming sorrows in life and brings spiritual enlightenment

How Hindus Worship & Puja Item

When Hindus worship at a shrine, it is called puja and it uses all five senses to help them feel close to God. First, they use their sense of smell by lighting incense sticks, which makes the room smell nice and feel peaceful. Next, they use their sense of touch by putting red kum kum powder on their foreheads for good luck and blessing and by counting mala beads while they say prayers. For their sense of sight, they do arti by waving a small arti lamp with a flame in front of the deity to show respect. Finally, after giving food offerings to the deity, they use their sense of taste by eating prashad, which is the special blessed food shared at the end.

Shaivite Shrines

Hinduism has several main branches—including Shaivism, Vaishnavism, Shaktism, Smartism, and groups like ISKCON—and each has its own style of shrine.

What is Shaivism?

Shaivism is a major branch of Hinduism where followers (Shaivites) worship Shiva as the Supreme Being. At a Shaivite shrine—either at home or in a temple (mandir)—worshippers perform daily prayers and rituals (puja) to find spiritual peace.

How is it different?

While all Hindu branches include chanting and festivals, Shaivism places a much stronger focus on meditation, yoga, and asceticism (simple, self-disciplined living). In whereas, branches like Shaktism focus more on chanting and large festivals.

Ayrton Fun Facts

  • Flower roots: The word puja likely comes from the ancient Tamil word pu, meaning "flower."
  • VIP treatment: Deities are treated like honoured guests—worshippers wake them, bathe them, dress them, and serve them meals.
  • All five senses: It engages sight (lamps), sound (bells), smell (incense), touch (sacred water), and taste (blessed food).
  • Blessed leftovers: Food offered during the ritual becomes prasad and is shared with everyone afterward.
  • Not just for gods: Special pujas also honour everyday things, including Kukur Tihar (a festival celebrating dogs) and Vishwakarma Puja (blessing tools and vehicles).
  • Eyes painted last: For big clay statues, artisans paint the eyes on in the dark at the very end to symbolically bring the deity to life.
