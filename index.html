Introduction to Puja

Puja is the Hindu act of worship. It is a way for Hindus to show love, respect, and devotion to God (Brahman) and the different deities, such as Ganesha, Lakshmi, or Shiva.

Puja can be performed every day at home in front of a family shrine, or together with the community at a Hindu temple called a Mandir. Before starting Puja, Hindus take off their shoes and wash to make sure they are clean and respectful.

A Hindu home shrine decorated with flowers and murtis (statues of deities).

Worship Using All Five Senses

Hindu worship is very sensory. Hindus believe that using all five senses helps them focus their minds completely on God:

  • Sight: Looking at the bright colours and statues (murtis) of the deities.
  • Sound: Ringing a bell to wake up the deity and chanting prayers (mantras).
  • Smell: Burning sweet-smelling incense sticks to purify the air.
  • Touch: Putting a red mark (tilak) on the forehead as a blessing.
  • Taste: Sharing blessed food (prashad) at the end of worship.

Items on a Puja Tray

During Puja, worshipers use a special metal tray containing important symbolic items:

🔔 Bell (Ghanta)

Rung at the start of Puja to invite the deity to be present and focus the worshiper's mind.

🕯️ Diva Lamp

A small oil lamp with a flame that represents light, knowledge, and God's presence.

🌿 Incense Stick

Lit to create a pleasant scent that fills the room and shows respect to the deity.

🔴 Kumkum Powder

Red paste used to place a dot (tilak) on the statue and the worshiper's forehead.

💧 Water & Spoon

Used to wash the murti and offer a drink to the deity as a sign of hospitality.

🍎 Food Offerings

Fruit, nuts, or sweets offered to God. Once blessed, it becomes prashad and is eaten.

A traditional Puja tray ready for daily worship.

Puja at Home vs. Puja at the Mandir

While most Hindus perform Puja every day at their home shrine, many also visit the Mandir (temple) for special festivals or weekly worship.

At the Mandir, a priest (Pandit) leads the prayers, and worshipers join together to sing hymns called bhajans. A special part of Mandir worship is Arti, where the Diva lamp is waved in front of the deities and then passed around so worshipers can cup their hands over the flame and bring the warmth to their foreheads as a blessing.
