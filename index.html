What is Puja?

Key Stage 3 Religious Education

Puja is the Hindu act of worship. It is a way for Hindus to show love and respect to God, creating a direct spiritual connection with the divine.

Where Worship Happens

  • The Home Shrine: A small altar where families pray together daily.
  • The Mandir: A temple where the community gathers for large services led by a priest (Brahmin).

Engaging the 5 Senses

Hindus use all five senses during Puja to focus completely on God:

1. Sight (Darshan): Looking into the eyes of the deity's statue (Murti).
2. Hearing: Ringing bells (Ghanti) and singing holy songs (Bhajans).
3. Smell: Burning incense sticks (Agarbatti) to purify the air.
4. Touch: Waving hands over the holy lamp flame to absorb its blessings.
5. Taste: Eating blessed food offerings called Prashad.

The Aarti Ceremony

The main part of modern Puja is the Aarti. A plate holding a small flame is rotated clockwise in front of the Murti while everyone sings prayers, plays music, and rings bells.

Why Puja Matters

  • It shows deep love and devotion (Bhakti) to God.
  • It brings families and the community closer together.
  • It helps fulfill a person's religious duty (Dharma) to build good Karma.
